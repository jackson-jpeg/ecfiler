Dear [Clerk of Court / ECF Help Desk],

I am a docketing specialist at [FIRM NAME] in Tampa, and I support attorneys filing
in this district daily. I have a question about the court's ECF procedures that I
have not been able to answer from the Administrative Procedures for Electronic
Filing, and I would be grateful for the court's guidance.

Our filings are prepared with software assistance — document validation, deadline
checks, and pre-filling of filing information — always operated by our staff, on our
own computers, under the supervision of the attorney of record, who reviews and
authorizes each filing before it is submitted under their login. No credentials are
shared with any outside service.

My question: does the court consider this kind of attorney-supervised,
software-assisted preparation and submission to fall within the ordinary use of an
attorney's ECF account by authorized staff under the court's ECF procedures? And is
there anything the court would want filers using such tools to do, or to avoid, to
stay squarely within its expectations?

We want to do this the way the court prefers. If a different office or person is the
right one for this question, I would appreciate a pointer.

Respectfully,

Jackson Sanger
Docketing Specialist, [FIRM NAME]
Tampa, Florida
[phone] · [email]

### Why the draft is framed this way

- It asks about *current practice* (staff-operated, attorney-supervised software on
  our own machines) — which is defensible and true post-remediation — not about
  hypothetical autonomy. A "yes, that's ordinary account use" answer is achievable
  and citable; a question about "AI agents" invites a no.
- It volunteers the two facts that matter to a clerk after 2023: no credential
  sharing with outside services, attorney authorizes each filing.
- It ends by asking what the court wants, which is the posture clerks respond to.
- M.D. Fla. note: verify at send time whether the district's Administrative
  Procedures have an "authorized agent"/staff-use provision to reference by section
  number — cite it if so, stay general if not. (Unverified at draft time.)
