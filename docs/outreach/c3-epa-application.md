## Statement of interest (draft)

I am a docketing specialist at [FIRM NAME]. PACER and CM/ECF are my working tools
every day: I locate and retrieve filings across dozens of districts, track deadlines
driven by NEFs, and prepare and support e-filings for attorneys across our offices.
People in my role are among the heaviest professional users of the Judiciary's
electronic public access services, and we experience its friction points — search
limitations, court-to-court variation, notice handling, fee mechanics — at volume and
in detail.

I am applying because the next two years are consequential ones for these systems. As
the Judiciary modernizes its case management and public access platforms, the
requirements gathered now will determine how well the replacement serves the
professionals who use it most intensively. I can contribute two things: first,
specific, current, high-volume user experience from inside a national firm's docketing
operation; second, a commitment to collecting and synthesizing feedback from my peers —
docketing and court-operations professionals whose perspective is underrepresented
relative to attorneys and commercial data vendors, and who are the people attorneys
actually delegate their electronic-access work to.

My interest is practical rather than commercial: clearer and more consistent access to
court records, notice, and filing status information, with security protections that
match how legal teams actually work — including reducing the informal credential
sharing that today's tools push busy practices toward. I would be glad to serve, to
attend the annual meeting in Washington, and to do the between-meeting work of
gathering peer feedback.

### Presentation decision (deliberate)

The application leads with the practitioner identity and mentions software work in one
line as "practitioner-developer" of open-source preparation tooling. ECFiler as a
product/company is *not* pitched: the group's charter seats user-community
representatives, the strongest cohort credential is high-frequency legal-sector usage,
and a commercial-vendor framing would compete for a different (already crowded) seat.
This is disclosure without salesmanship — if the form asks directly about commercial
interests, answer fully and plainly.
